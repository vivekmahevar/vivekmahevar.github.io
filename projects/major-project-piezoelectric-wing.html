Comparative Analysis of Piezoelectric Materials

In a Sandwich Aircraft Wing

A comparative finite element study investigating five piezoelectric materials integrated into a sandwich aircraft wing for vibration energy harvesting and structural health monitoring applications.

01
RESEARCH PROBLEM

Can a structural wing also harvest useful vibration energy?

Aircraft wings experience vibration caused by aerodynamic forces, turbulence, engine excitation, and aeroelastic effects. Piezoelectric materials can convert mechanical strain into electrical energy through the direct piezoelectric effect.

The research investigates how different PZT ceramic materials behave when integrated into the sandwich skin of an aircraft wing, considering both structural and electromechanical performance.

The central objective was to identify a material that provides a useful balance between structural behaviour, dynamic characteristics, and electrical potential generation.

02

Five commercially available Lead Zirconate Titanate ceramics were incorporated into the same sandwich-wing configuration and evaluated under consistent modelling and analysis conditions.

03
AIRCRAFT WING MODEL

A three-dimensional sandwich half-wing built in CATIA V5.

A rectangular half-wing based on the Clark-Y airfoil was developed in CATIA V5. The model was designed as a cantilever structure with internal spars and ribs supporting a segmented sandwich skin.

CONFIGURATION Rectangular Half-Wing
AIRFOIL Clark-Y
HALF SPAN 500 mm
ROOT / TIP CHORD 115.84 mm
AIRFOIL THICKNESS 13.55 mm
TOTAL SANDWICH THICKNESS 3 mm

The wing structure contains two hollow square spars and six ribs. The sandwich skin uses aluminium outer layers with a segmented intermediate layer.

04
SANDWICH STRUCTURE

Embedding the active material inside the wing skin.

The sandwich skin consists of aluminium face sheets surrounding a segmented intermediate layer. Instead of replacing the complete intermediate layer with piezoelectric material, the active region is concentrated into six PZT segments.

01 Upper Face Sheet Aluminium · 1 mm
02 Intermediate Layer 8 chordwise segments · 6 PZT + 2 aluminium
03 Lower Face Sheet Aluminium · 1 mm

The six central PZT segments are positioned in the active region of the sandwich structure so that mechanical strain can be transferred into the piezoelectric layer.

05
MATERIAL SELECTION

Five materials. One identical structural model.

The comparison was designed so that geometry, meshing, contacts, loading, and boundary conditions remained consistent while the piezoelectric material was varied.

PZT-4 Hard Ceramic

High stiffness and low dielectric loss.

PZT-5A Soft Ceramic

High sensitivity with balanced electromechanical properties.

PZT-5H Soft Ceramic

High piezoelectric coefficient and coupling characteristics.

PZT-8 Hard Ceramic

High mechanical quality factor and durability.

PZT-26 Modified PZT

Balanced mechanical and electrical characteristics.

07
LOADING & BOUNDARY CONDITIONS

Keeping every material under the same conditions.

A fixed support was applied at the wing root to represent the attachment to the aircraft fuselage.

A dynamic pressure of 250 Pa was applied to the lower wing surface for the static structural analysis. The same conditions were retained across all material cases.

PRESSURE 250 Pa
PRESSURE LOCATION Lower Wing Surface
SUPPORT Fixed at Wing Root
HARMONIC RANGE 0 – 1160.3 Hz

For the coupled-field harmonic analysis, the bottom PZT surface was assigned 0 V ground potential while the top surface was used to determine the generated electrical potential.

Static structural analysis evaluated total deformation, equivalent von Mises stress, and equivalent elastic strain for each material.

MODAL ANALYSIS

PZT-26 achieved the highest fundamental frequency.

Modal analysis was used to determine the natural frequencies and mode shapes of the wing. The first six modes were extracted for each material configuration.

HIGHEST FUNDAMENTAL FREQUENCY 54.193 Hz PZT-26 · Mode 1

Under the same model conditions, PZT-26 produced the highest first natural frequency, indicating the strongest dynamic stiffness among the investigated configurations.

11
OVERALL PERFORMANCE

No single material dominates every parameter.

The final comparison considered structural, dynamic, and electromechanical performance together rather than selecting a material using electrical output alone.

BEST STATIC STIFFNESS PZT-26

0.1387 mm minimum static deflection.

BEST DYNAMIC STIFFNESS PZT-26

54.193 Hz fundamental frequency.

HIGHEST VOLTAGE PZT-8

6275.1 mV predicted peak voltage.

OVERALL CANDIDATE PZT-26

Strong structural and dynamic behaviour with comparatively high voltage output.

CONCLUSION FROM THE NUMERICAL STUDY PZT-26 was identified as the most favourable material for the investigated smart sandwich aircraft wing configuration.

PZT-8 produced the highest predicted electrical voltage, while PZT-26 provided the lowest static deformation and highest fundamental frequency.

12
ENGINEERING INTERPRETATION

The material choice depends on what the structure must do.

The results demonstrate that electrical output alone is not sufficient for selecting a piezoelectric material for a smart aircraft structure.

PZT-8 generated the highest predicted voltage, but PZT-26 produced the lowest static deformation and the highest fundamental natural frequency. Its predicted voltage of 6117.6 mV was also close to the maximum obtained with PZT-8.

Under the investigated modelling conditions, the combined structural, dynamic, and electrical results therefore favoured PZT-26.

17
PROJECT TAKEAWAY

Smart structures require both mechanical and electrical thinking.

This project strengthened my understanding of aircraft structural behaviour, finite element analysis, modal dynamics, and piezoelectric electromechanical coupling.

More importantly, it demonstrated how material selection becomes a multi-objective engineering problem when a structural component is expected to carry loads while also performing sensing or energy-harvesting functions.

FINAL NUMERICAL FINDING PZT-26 offered the most favourable combined structural and dynamic performance for the investigated sandwich-wing model.
