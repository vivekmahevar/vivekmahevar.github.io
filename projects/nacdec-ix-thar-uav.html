THE DESIGN CHALLENGE

Designing around a mission, not just an aircraft.

NACDeC-IX presented a system-level conceptual aircraft design problem. The THAR UAV had to operate from a prepared desert area without a conventional runway, remain transportable, sustain long-duration flight, and carry its specified mission payload.

These requirements created interconnected design constraints across aircraft sizing, aerodynamics, propulsion, stability, weight, and configuration.

01 Runway Independent

Operation from a 50 × 50 m prepared area.

02 Long Endurance

More than 9 hours of total flight duration.

03 High Altitude

3000 m AMSL cruise requirement.

04 Transportable

Designed around a portable, dismantlable system.

05 Weight Limited

Competition MTOW constraint below 175 kg.

06 Compact Span

Maximum permitted wingspan below 6 m.

02
DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

A balanced aircraft emerged from iterative trade-offs.

The aircraft was approached as a combination of interconnected systems rather than as an isolated aerodynamic design exercise.

Mission requirements were translated into preliminary aircraft characteristics, followed by configuration, aerodynamic, propulsion, weight, and stability considerations. Each decision was evaluated against its effects on the overall aircraft concept.

From mission constraints to aircraft scale.

Preliminary aircraft sizing was performed using established conceptual design methodologies from Raymer and J.D. Anderson.

The sizing process connected the mission requirements with aircraft weight, wing characteristics, propulsion requirements, and endurance targets to establish a feasible baseline configuration.

PROJECT TAKEAWAY

Aircraft design is a systems problem.

NACDeC-IX strengthened my understanding of how mission requirements propagate through an aircraft design. Aerodynamics, weight, stability, propulsion, and configuration cannot be treated independently; improving one aspect can introduce constraints somewhere else.

The project gave me practical experience in turning an open-ended aerospace mission into a structured conceptual design process supported by engineering analysis and technical justification.
