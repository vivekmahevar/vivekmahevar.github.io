National Aeromodelling Competition – Techfest IIT Bombay

Developed a lightweight fixed-wing RC aircraft for the National Aeromodelling Competition at Techfest, IIT Bombay, integrating aircraft sizing, flight mechanics, stability analysis, structural analysis, CFD, payload design, and fabrication.

01
PROJECT OVERVIEW

Designing around a payload-focused mission

The project involved the development of a fixed-wing RC aircraft for the National Aeromodelling Competition conducted during Techfest at IIT Bombay.

The aircraft was designed around the competition's requirements for payload carrying, stable flight, safe operation, and efficient mission execution.

The final aircraft had an estimated mass of 972.97 g without payload, a wingspan of 1.16 m, and an estimated payload capacity of 1.44 kg.

02
DESIGN CHALLENGE

Balancing size, weight and mission capability

The competition required teams to design and fabricate their own fixed-wing RC aircraft within defined limits on aircraft weight, wingspan, propulsion, and propeller size.

The aircraft also needed to carry its payload internally and complete the required flight mission safely without automated flight assistance.

These constraints made aircraft configuration, weight management, stability, and payload integration important parts of the design process.

03
AIRCRAFT CONFIGURATION

High-wing configuration with Clark-Y airfoil

A rectangular high-wing configuration with a conventional tail was selected for the aircraft.

The Clark-Y airfoil was selected for its low-speed lift characteristics, stable behaviour, and practical suitability for the construction of a small aircraft.

The wing had a span of 1160 mm, a surface area of 197200 mm², and a constant chord of 170 mm.

04
AIRCRAFT SIZING

Sizing the aircraft around its mission

Initial sizing covered the wing, fuselage, and empennage while considering the aircraft weight, payload requirement, and flight characteristics.

The fuselage was designed with an overall length of 720 mm, extending to approximately 850 mm including the empennage.

Centre-of-gravity estimation and aircraft weight calculations were used to evaluate the resulting configuration before analysis.

05
PROPULSION SYSTEM

Electric propulsion sized for the aircraft

The aircraft used an electric propulsion system selected according to the competition constraints and the aircraft's performance requirements.

The selected system consisted of an A2212 1400 KV motor, a 30 A ESC, a 3S 2200 mAh battery, and a 9.5 × 4.5 propeller.

Propulsion performance was evaluated through thrust and power behaviour across the aircraft's operating velocity range.

06
PAYLOAD SYSTEM

Integrated internal payload mechanism

The aircraft incorporated an internal payload compartment designed around the competition's requirement that the payload remain enclosed within the aircraft.

The design was intended to carry up to 32 golf balls using an inclined internal arrangement within the fuselage.

A servo-operated side door was incorporated to release the payload, while a top opening allowed loading from the ground.

07
ENGINEERING ANALYSIS

Structural integrity and aerodynamic evaluation

Static structural analysis was performed on the wing to evaluate equivalent stress and total deformation under the defined loading condition.

CFD analysis was performed using ANSYS Fluent to examine the aerodynamic flow around the aircraft through velocity and pressure contour analysis.

The analysis results were used as part of the engineering evaluation of the aircraft configuration.

08
STABILITY ANALYSIS

Evaluating the aircraft response to disturbances

Longitudinal static stability was evaluated through pitching-moment behaviour with respect to angle of attack.

Longitudinal dynamic stability was investigated through pitch rate, pitch angle, and velocity responses following a disturbance.

Lateral dynamic stability was also evaluated through roll, bank-angle, yaw, and velocity responses to disturbances.

09
FLIGHT PERFORMANCE

Aerodynamic performance across the flight envelope

The aircraft performance evaluation considered take-off, cruise, and stall velocities together with lift, drag, and aerodynamic coefficient behaviour.

The calculated take-off velocity was 15.35 m/s, the cruise velocity was 22.15 m/s, and the stall velocity was 12.79 m/s.

Aerodynamic plots including lift coefficient, drag coefficient, lift-to-drag ratio, drag breakdown, and flight envelope were used to assess the aircraft's operating characteristics.

10
FABRICATION

Lightweight construction for the final aircraft

Balsa wood was used for the ribs and bulkheads because of its favourable strength-to-weight characteristics.

Aluminium rods were used for the wing spars and fuselage structural members to maintain a lightweight structure.

Laser cutting was used to produce the required rib and bulkhead geometries before assembly of the aircraft.

11
MY CONTRIBUTION

Aircraft design and engineering analysis

My contribution covered aircraft configuration development, aircraft sizing, centre-of-gravity estimation, and stability evaluation.

I also contributed to the aerodynamic and structural evaluation of the aircraft through CFD and static structural analysis.

The design was iteratively evaluated using engineering calculations and simulation results to refine the overall aircraft configuration.

14
ENGINEERING TAKEAWAY

From aircraft concept to a mission-capable system

This project brought together aircraft configuration, sizing, aerodynamic analysis, structural evaluation, stability, payload integration, and physical fabrication within a single aircraft development process.

It provided practical experience in applying aerospace engineering principles to a complete fixed-wing aircraft rather than analysing individual components in isolation.
