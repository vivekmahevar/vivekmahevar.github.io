01
THE CHALLENGE

Designing around a demanding competition mission

The Micro Class mission required teams to develop an all-electric fixed-wing aircraft capable of carrying a liquid payload while satisfying strict operational and dimensional constraints.

The design challenge involved balancing payload capability, take-off performance, aircraft weight, stability, aerodynamic performance, and practical manufacturability.

LIQUID PAYLOAD 67 fl oz
MAXIMUM TAKE-OFF WEIGHT 55 lb
BATTERY LIMIT 4-cell LiPo
TAKE-OFF DISTANCE 100 ft

LANDING LIMIT 200 ft
02
DESIGN APPROACH

From mission requirements to aircraft configuration

The aircraft configuration was developed through design trade studies considering aerodynamic performance, stability, structural weight, manufacturability, and payload integration.

The selected configuration used a tapered high wing, a cambered airfoil-based fuselage, conventional empennage, and puller-tricycle landing gear.

WING Tapered High Wing
FUSELAGE Cambered Airfoil Section
EMPENNAGE Conventional Tail
LANDING GEAR Puller Tricycle

AERODYNAMIC DESIGN

Airfoil evaluation and aerodynamic development

Multiple airfoil candidates were evaluated based on aerodynamic characteristics, stability considerations, performance, and manufacturability.

The final wing profile was developed through interpolation of selected airfoil geometries. Aerodynamic behaviour was subsequently evaluated through computational analysis.

05
ENGINEERING ANALYSIS

Analytical and computational validation

The aircraft design was evaluated using a combination of analytical calculations and computational engineering tools covering aerodynamics, structures, stability, payload behaviour, and aircraft performance.

01
Structural Analysis

ANSYS-based evaluation of the wing and landing gear to investigate structural response under the defined loading conditions.

02
Computational Fluid Dynamics

ANSYS Fluent was used to investigate aircraft aerodynamic behaviour through computational flow analysis.

03
Payload Analysis

Multiphase analysis was used to study liquid movement inside the payload bay and investigate the effect of baffle configurations.

04
Stability & Performance

Static and dynamic stability together with take-off, cruise, stall, range, endurance, and flight-envelope performance were evaluated.

06
CAD & FABRICATION

Translating the digital design into an aircraft

The aircraft was developed using CAD tools before fabrication. AutoCAD was used to prepare laser-cutting layouts, while CATIA V5 and Fusion 360 supported the aircraft modelling process.

The prototype combined lightweight materials with practical manufacturing techniques including laser cutting, hot-wire cutting, drilling, and 3D printing.

07
PROTOTYPE & TESTING

Validating the aircraft through physical testing

A full-scale prototype was fabricated and prepared for flight testing. The physical testing stage provided practical validation of the aircraft configuration and revealed areas requiring further refinement.

The testing experience connected the analytical design process with the behaviour of the physical aircraft and provided practical feedback for engineering decisions.

PROJECT TAKEAWAY

From requirements to a flying aircraft

This project brought together aircraft sizing, aerodynamic design, stability, computational analysis, CAD, fabrication, and flight testing within a single aircraft development cycle.

It provided practical experience in understanding how interconnected engineering decisions influence the final aircraft and how analytical work must ultimately translate into a manufacturable and testable flying system.
